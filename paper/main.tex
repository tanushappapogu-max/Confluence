\begin{abstract}
Retrieval and computation are usually two glued-together stages: search a memory, then run a
fixed reasoning stack, with nothing preventing illegal logical jumps. We make look-up and
computation the \emph{same} act. We place every fact and every compute-expert as nodes of a
single per-query graph whose edges are exactly the legal transitions, and answer a query by
routing \emph{one} differentiable, min-cost \emph{conserved flow} (a Tero--Nakagaki Physarum
dynamic) through it. The flow relaxes to the cheapest \emph{legal} path, which retrieves at data
nodes and computes at expert nodes in one connected sweep. Two guarantees fall out of the
mechanism: \emph{legality by topology} (a constraint violation is impossible, not penalized,
because there is no edge where there is no triple) and \emph{conservation coupling} (flow-in
equals flow-out, coupling consecutive steps). We show the mechanism is real, differentiable, and
trainable; that on MetaQA 2-hop it reaches \TODO{hits@1} with a hard 0-hallucination guarantee;
and that the flow can be trained by implicit differentiation through its fixed point, removing the
cost that otherwise restricts it to small neighborhoods.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Standard retrieval-augmented pipelines take the top-$k$ most similar facts and \emph{average}
them (a lossy ``averaging ceiling''), then feed a fixed stack; nothing enforces that reasoning
steps are legal. We collapse retrieval and computation into a single min-cost legal path through
one graph. Contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A conserved-flow / Physarum dynamic used as the \emph{router itself} (\S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item \textbf{Hard, input-conditional legality by topology}: illegal transitions are impossible,
        not merely rare (\S\ref{sec:method}, \S\ref{sec:exp}).
  \item \textbf{Unification of retrieval and computation} in one flow through data and weight nodes.
  \item \textbf{Scalability}: implicit differentiation through the flow fixed point and a
        matrix-free forward solve, verified to machine precision (\S\ref{sec:scaling}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Graph and costs.} A signed incidence $B\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times E}$ encodes the per-query
legal graph. An encoder maps the input to per-edge logits $z$; edge cost $L_e=\mathrm{softplus}(-z_e)+\varepsilon$.
\paragraph{Conserved-flow dynamic.} With conductance $D_e$ and weight $w_e=D_e/L_e$, flux
$Q_e=w_e(B^\top p)_e$ obeys Kirchhoff conservation $\mathcal{L}(w)\,p=b$, where
$\mathcal{L}(w)=B\,\mathrm{diag}(w)\,B^\top$ is the weighted Laplacian and $b$ injects $+1$ at the
source and $-1$ at the sink (grounded). Reinforcement $D_e\leftarrow D_e+\Delta t(|Q_e|-D_e)$
iterates to the fixed point $D_e^\star=|Q_e^\star|$, at which a single sparse path survives.
\paragraph{Selection and composition.} Node throughput $t_v=\tfrac12\sum_e|B_{v,e}||Q_e|$;
per position $a_{k}=t^\gamma/\sum t^\gamma$. Walking the path, data nodes inject a value and
expert nodes apply an MLP, giving function \emph{composition} (not a weighted average).
\paragraph{Two guarantees.} $e\notin E\Rightarrow Q_e=0$ (legality by topology); and
$\sum_{\text{in}}Q=\sum_{\text{out}}Q$ (conservation coupling).

\section{Scalability: differentiating and solving the flow}\label{sec:scaling}
The solve is one implicit layer $z^\star=F(z^\star,\theta)$ with $F(D)=|Q(D)|$. By the implicit
function theorem, for cotangent $g=\partial\mathcal{J}/\partial z^\star$ the parameter gradient is
$\partial\mathcal{J}/\partial\theta=(\partial F/\partial\theta)^\top u$ with $(I-(\partial F/\partial z)^\top)u=g$,
solved matrix-free (VJP-only). This gives $O(E)$ backward memory independent of solver depth.
We verify the implicit gradient against finite differences (cosine $1.0$, rel-err $\sim\!10^{-8}$)
and against the exact Jacobian; a small conductance floor regularizes the otherwise-degenerate
hard fixed point. A matrix-free conjugate-gradient forward solve (Laplacian-vector products from
the edge list, $O(E)$/iter) replaces the dense $O(N^3)$ solve, matching it to $\sim\!10^{-3}$ and
solving $N\!\approx\!2400$ neighborhoods in $\sim\!0.4$s.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
\paragraph{Mechanism (synthetic).} A single optimal path emerges and alternatives wither; $14$
redundant edges collapse to $2$; gradient through the fixed point re-routes the path.
\paragraph{The legality wedge.} On a mixed data+compute task, Confluence attains illegal-hop rate
$\approx\!0$ by construction while a PathMoE-style shared-router baseline attains the \emph{worst}
legality under noise (illegal-hop $\approx\!0.5$): soft/param-sharing constraints can make legality
worse, not just fail to guarantee it.

\section{Related work}
Each neighbor owns one ingredient; the conjunction is empty. PathMoE (path-constrained MoE,
soft/statistical, weights only); Chain-of-Experts (sequential composition, learned router);
GFlowNet-for-KG (flow sampling, not min-cost, data only); PathRAG (non-parametric flow pruning);
D-RAG (differentiable subgraph \emph{set}); MoG (heuristic topology router); Physarum diff-LP
layers (LPs, not expert routing). We concede the scooped \emph{outcomes} and claim the mechanism
(flow-as-router), the hard input-conditional legality guarantee, and the retrieval/computation
unification.

\section{Limitations}
The legality source is the KG (clean on KGQA, open where no native graph exists); $\gamma$ and the
conductance floor are tuned (we report sensitivity); results so far are single-domain (movies) ---
WebQSP/CWQ are needed for external validity.
